> On-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student on its *own* rollouts, graded token by token by a teacher. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) trains on fixed labels. They look like different recipes, but SFT is exactly a corner case of the same loss. Let me try to formalize this.

Let $V$ be a finite vocabulary containing an end-of-sequence token, and $\Delta(V)$ the probability simplex over it. A prompt $x$ is drawn from a prompt distribution $\rho$ (in practice, uniform over a dataset). A response is a token sequence $y = (y_1, \dots, y_{|y|})$ ending at the first end-of-sequence token, and

$$h_n = (x, y_{<n})$$

is the *context* at step $n$: everything the model sees before emitting $y_n$.

An autoregressive policy $\pi$ maps every context to a next-token distribution $\pi(\cdot \mid h) \in \Delta(V)$, so that 

$$\pi(y \mid x) = \prod_{n=1}^{|y|} \pi(y_n \mid h_n).$$ 

We work with two policies:

> the **student** $p_S = p_\theta$, trainable (a softmax, so $p_S(v \mid h) > 0$ for every $v$ and $h$);

> the **teacher** $p_T$, fixed. Being defined on every context, it can be evaluated on contexts produced by any policy, in particular by the student.

For a divergence $D$ on $\Delta(V)$ (with $D(p \,\Vert\, q) \geq 0$, equality iff $p = q$), the mismatch on a full response averages over its positions, with the teacher as first argument:

$$D(p_T \,\|\, p_S)(y \mid x) = \frac{1}{|y|} \sum_{n=1}^{|y|} D\big(p_T(\cdot \mid h_n) \,\|\, p_S(\cdot \mid h_n)\big).$$

#### On-policy vs. off-policy

Let $\mu$ be the *behavioural policy*, the one that generates the responses on which the loss is evaluated. The loss is

$$\mathcal{L}_\mu(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \rho} \, \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \mu(\cdot \mid x)} \Big[ D(p_T \,\|\, p_S)(y \mid x) \Big].$$

> **On-policy** (OPD): $\mu = p_S$. The contexts are the ones the student visits, and they change with $\theta$.

> **Off-policy**: $\mu$ is a fixed policy independent of $\theta$. We take $\mu = p_T$: the student is evaluated on contexts generated by the teacher.

The two losses have the same integrand and differ only in who generates the contexts. The distribution mismatch between training contexts and the contexts the student sees at inference is the classic motivation for the on-policy version [3, 4].

#### Forward (direct) KL

Choose 

$$D(p \,\|\, q) = D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p \,\|\, q) = \sum_v p(v) \log \frac{p(v)}{q(v)}\, ,$$ 

with $0 \log 0 = 0$. At a single context it splits into a teacher entropy and a cross-entropy:

$$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_T \,\|\, p_S) = \underbrace{\sum_v p_T(v) \log p_T(v)}_{-H(p_T)} \; \underbrace{- \sum_v p_T(v) \log p_S(v)}_{H(p_T,\, p_S)}.$$

> **On-policy.** For a fixed context, $H(p_T(\cdot \mid h))$ does not depend on $\theta$. But the contexts $h_n$ are generated by $p_S = p_\theta$, so the *average* of this entropy over them still depends on $\theta$. The on-policy forward-KL loss is therefore not just a cross-entropy.

> **Off-policy.** With $y \sim p_T$, the contexts do not depend on $\theta$, so

$$\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{fwd}}_{\mathrm{off}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_x \, \mathbb{E}_{y \sim p_T} \Big[ \frac{1}{|y|} \sum_{n} H\big(p_T(\cdot \mid h_n),\, p_S(\cdot \mid h_n)\big) \Big] - C,$$

$$C = \mathbb{E}_x \, \mathbb{E}_{y \sim p_T} \Big[ \frac{1}{|y|} \sum_{n} H\big(p_T(\cdot \mid h_n)\big) \Big],$$

and $C$ does not depend on $\theta$. Off-policy, the forward KL is a cross-entropy against the teacher's soft labels, up to a constant. This is the usual word-level knowledge distillation [1], evaluated along teacher-generated sequences.

A word on why forward KL: its terms are weighted by $p_T(v)$. If $p_T(v) > 0$ and $p_S(v) \to 0$, the term diverges, so the student must put mass wherever the teacher does. A student that cannot represent $p_T$ therefore spreads its mass over all modes of $p_T$: forward KL is *mass-covering* [5].

#### The hard-teacher limit is SFT

A *hard* teacher is deterministic: there is a map $a$ from contexts to tokens with

$$p_T(v \mid h) = \delta_{v,\, a(h)} \quad \text{for every context } h.$$

Generating with it from a prompt $x$ gives a single response, the *label*,

$$y(x) = (y_1, y_2, \dots), \qquad y_n = a(x, y_{<n}),$$

so $y \sim p_T(\cdot \mid x)$ is a point mass on $y(x)$. At each context along it, the sum over $v$ collapses onto $v = y_n$, and since $p_T(y_n \mid h_n) = 1$,

$$\sum_{v} \delta_{v,y_n} \log \frac{\delta_{v,y_n}}{p_S(v \mid h_n)} = -\log p_S(y_n \mid h_n) = H(\delta_{y_n}, p_S).$$

Since $H(\delta_{y_n}) = 0$, we also have $C = 0$. The off-policy loss becomes

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SFT}}(\theta) = -\,\mathbb{E}_x \Big[ \frac{1}{|y|} \sum_{n=1}^{|y|} \log p_S(y_n \mid x, y_{<n}) \Big],$$

the cross-entropy of the student against a hard label, i.e. SFT. If the labels are the dataset's correct solutions $y^\star$ (that is, $a(x, y^\star_{<n}) = y^\star_n$ along the prefixes of $y^\star$), then $y(x) = y^\star$ and the average runs over the dataset pairs $(x, y^\star)$.

In practice, sequence-level distillation [2] is close to this limit: the student is trained on the teacher's beam-search output, one hard label per prompt.

Setting $p_T = \delta_a$ directly is a substitution. To see it is a limit, mix the hard teacher with a little uniform noise:

$$p_T^{\varepsilon}(v \mid h) = (1 - \varepsilon)\, \delta_{v,a(h)} + \frac{\varepsilon}{|V|}, \qquad \varepsilon \in (0, 1).$$

At a single context,

$$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_T^{\varepsilon} \,\|\, p_S) = \underbrace{\sum_v p_T^{\varepsilon}(v) \log p_T^{\varepsilon}(v)}_{\to\, 0} \; \underbrace{- \sum_v p_T^{\varepsilon}(v) \log p_S(v)}_{\to\, -\log p_S(a \mid h)}.$$

The first term tends to $0$ because $t \log t \to 0$ both as $t \to 0$ and as $t \to 1$, the only values the probabilities $p_T^{\varepsilon}(v)$ approach. The second tends to $-\log p_S(a \mid h)$ because $p_T^{\varepsilon} \to \delta_a$ and each $\log p_S(v \mid h)$ is a finite number ($p_S > 0$). So

$$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_T^{\varepsilon} \,\|\, p_S) \xrightarrow{\;\varepsilon \to 0\;} -\log p_S(a \mid h).$$

The contexts are also sampled from $p_T^{\varepsilon}$. Let $L$ be the maximal response length. With probability at least $(1 - \varepsilon)^L \to 1$ the sampled response is the label $y(x)$, and all other responses have vanishing probability and bounded divergence (again because $p_S > 0$). So, for fixed $\theta$, the full off-policy loss converges to $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SFT}}$.

#### Reverse KL has no hard-teacher limit

Now swap the arguments. At a context $h$, with the hard teacher $p_T = \delta_a$:

$$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_T \,\|\, p_S) = -\log p_S(a \mid h) \quad \text{(finite)},$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_S \,\|\, p_T) &= \sum_v p_S(v \mid h) \log \frac{p_S(v \mid h)}{\delta_{v,a}} \\
&= +\infty \quad \text{if } p_S(v \mid h) > 0 \text{ for some } v \neq a.
\end{aligned}
$$

In the reverse KL each term is weighted by $p_S(v)$, and any token $v \neq a$ on which the student puts mass contributes $p_S(v) \log \frac{p_S(v)}{0} = +\infty$. A softmax student has $p_S(v \mid h) > 0$ for every $v$, so the reverse-KL loss is $+\infty$ for every student. With the smoothed teacher, the divergence is explicit:

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_S \,\|\, p_T^{\varepsilon}) &= -H(p_S) - p_S(a) \log\Big(1 - \varepsilon + \frac{\varepsilon}{|V|}\Big) + \big(1 - p_S(a)\big) \log \frac{|V|}{\varepsilon} \\
&\xrightarrow{\;\varepsilon \to 0\;} +\infty.
\end{aligned}
$$

The first term does not depend on $\varepsilon$, the second tends to $0$, and the third diverges because $p_S(a) < 1$.

So SFT, a cross-entropy against a hard label, comes from the **forward** KL and not from the reverse KL. This matters because the reverse KL is the usual choice for on-policy distillation: it is mode-seeking, which suits a student that cannot represent the teacher [5], and it can be read as a per-token reward for the student's own samples [6].

| Behavioural policy | Divergence | Teacher | What the loss is |
|---|---|---|---|
| off-policy ($p_T$) | forward KL | soft | cross-entropy with soft labels, up to a constant [1] |
| off-policy ($p_T$) | forward KL | hard | **SFT** (cross-entropy with one-hot labels) |
| on-policy ($p_S$) | forward KL | soft | cross-entropy plus a $\theta$-dependent entropy term |
| on-policy ($p_S$) | reverse KL | soft | OPD as used in practice [5, 6]; no hard-teacher limit |

Remarks:
> **The sampling is usually not differentiated.** The on-policy loss depends on $\theta$ both through $p_S$ in the integrand and through the sampling distribution. Implementations typically treat the sampled rollouts as constants (stop-gradient). In that case the entropy term has zero gradient, and the on-policy forward-KL update is the gradient of the cross-entropy against the teacher's soft labels along student rollouts.
> **Normalization.** The per-sequence average $1/|y|$ above weights short and long responses equally. Many SFT implementations instead average over all tokens in the batch. This changes the weighting of sequences but not the structure of the result.
> **Where the corner sits.** In terms of sampling and reward density: SFT is off-policy with dense per-token signal, RL is on-policy with sparse signal, and OPD is on-policy with dense signal [6]. The derivation above shows SFT as the point of the OPD family where the behavioural policy is the teacher, the divergence is forward KL, and the teacher is hard.
